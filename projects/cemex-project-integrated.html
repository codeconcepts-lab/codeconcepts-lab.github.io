Project Overview

When Your Operations Are Enterprise-Grade, Your Digital Presence Has to Match

In the Nigerian construction and infrastructure sector, corporate website quality is a credibility signal — one that prospective clients and institutional partners evaluate before a single meeting is scheduled. Cemex Project Integrated had built a reputation for serious project delivery across construction, procurement, facility management, and industrial services. But its digital presence wasn't communicating the depth of that capability to the audiences who needed to see it.

Nexora was brought in to do more than redesign a website — the mandate was to engineer a digital corporate identity that matched the company's operational reality. That meant building a platform capable of clearly presenting a multi-service portfolio, establishing enterprise credibility through visual and structural sophistication, and creating a scalable information architecture that could grow with the business without requiring structural rebuilds.

  • Replace a fragmented digital presence with a structured, authoritative corporate platform
  • Create a service communication system that clearly presents multiple verticals without overwhelming visitors
  • Establish visual credibility signals that position the company appropriately for institutional and enterprise client engagement
  • Build a scalable backend architecture that allows content teams to expand the platform as the business grows

The Challenge

Communicating Enterprise-Scale Capability Without Losing Clarity or Credibility.

Infrastructure and construction companies face a specific UX challenge that consumer brands do not: their audiences are sophisticated, time-constrained, and evaluating multiple vendors simultaneously. A corporate website in this sector isn't marketing collateral — it's a due diligence tool. Procurement managers, project owners, and institutional clients arrive at a corporate website with specific questions, and if those questions aren't answered quickly and credibly, the company is deprioritised before the relationship has even begun.

Multi-Service Communication Complexity

Cemex Project Integrated's service portfolio spanned multiple distinct verticals — construction and civil works, procurement and supply chain, facility management, and industrial services. Presenting these on a single corporate platform without creating confusion, visual clutter, or navigation paralysis required a service architecture strategy that could make each vertical feel appropriately prominent without competing with the others for attention. This is a significantly harder information design problem than single-service corporate sites face.

Credibility Signalling in a High-Stakes Decision Environment

In B2B infrastructure services, the visual quality and structural sophistication of a company's digital presence is interpreted as a proxy for operational quality. A poorly structured website in this sector doesn't just fail to impress — it actively damages the credibility assessment of an otherwise capable company. The existing platform wasn't projecting the confidence or capability that the company's track record warranted, creating a discrepancy that was costing them in first-impression evaluations.

Scalability Without Architectural Debt

Infrastructure businesses expand their service offerings over time. A platform built without scalability in mind becomes an obstacle to growth — content teams are forced to work around structural limitations, new services can't be added cleanly, and the eventual cost of a rebuild far exceeds what good architecture would have required upfront. Cemex Project Integrated needed a CMS and page architecture that could accommodate growth without technical debt accumulation.

"In B2B infrastructure services, a company's digital presence is evaluated like a portfolio submission. Clients aren't just reading your content — they're using its quality to infer your operational standards. The website is the first deliverable."

Strategy & Discovery

Credibility Architecture as the Foundation of Every Design Decision

Nexora's discovery process for Cemex Project Integrated began with a stakeholder audience analysis — mapping the distinct decision-maker profiles who would visit the platform and understanding what each of them needed to see, in what order, to reach a positive credibility assessment. The architecture wasn't designed for a generic visitor. It was designed for a procurement officer evaluating a shortlist, a project owner validating a referral, and a facilities manager researching service capabilities. Each of these audiences had different entry points and different information requirements.

Authority-First Homepage Architecture

The homepage was designed as a credibility statement, not a sales pitch. Above the fold: the company's positioning and sector authority, communicated through visual confidence and typographic weight. Immediately below: proof — project categories, operational scope, and service breadth — structured so a time-constrained visitor could assess capability within thirty seconds without scrolling to secondary content. Authority established first. Detail available below.

Service Architecture: Hierarchy Without Competition

The service presentation system was designed around a tiered disclosure model. Primary service categories were surfaced at the navigation and homepage levels, each with a clear summary and its own dedicated page. Within each service page, the architecture moved from broad capability statement to specific deliverables to relevant project examples. This structure allowed each vertical to be appropriately prominent without fragmenting the overall brand narrative or creating navigation complexity that would confuse visitors who arrived from a specific service search.

Conversion Through Clarity

Corporate B2B conversions are not impulse decisions. The goal was not to generate immediate form submissions but to build sufficient credibility and clarity that a qualified prospect would choose to take the next step — whether that was requesting a consultation, downloading a capability document, or making a direct inquiry. Every CTA on the platform was positioned as a logical next action for a visitor who had already completed a natural information-gathering journey, rather than an interruption in that journey.

Visual Design System

Authority Through Precision — An Enterprise Design Language

The visual language for Cemex Project Integrated was calibrated for a specific audience response: confidence. Not excitement, not warmth — confidence. The design system needed to communicate that this is a company that executes at scale, delivers on time, and operates with the precision that large-scale infrastructure projects demand. Every design decision was evaluated against that standard. Elements that introduced uncertainty, visual noise, or consumer-brand associations were removed. Elements that communicated precision, structure, and depth were amplified.

Colour System

Typography

The typographic system was built for information density without visual fatigue. Display headings used a high-weight geometric sans — modern, structured, confident — appropriate for a company operating in precision-demanding industrial sectors. Body copy was set at a size and line-height combination calibrated for comfortable reading of detailed service descriptions, a necessity when potential clients are conducting comparative due diligence across multiple corporate platforms. Supporting metadata — project specifications, service categories, geographic scope — used a condensed variant for efficient information delivery.

Design Principles

Structure as a Trust Signal

In corporate B2B design, visual organisation communicates operational organisation. A company that presents complex multi-service information in a clear, navigable structure is implicitly demonstrating the same capability it claims to apply to physical projects. Structural clarity is not just a UX requirement — it is a brand statement.

Precision Over Decoration

Enterprise clients are not responding to aesthetic appeal — they are assessing capability and reliability. The design system prioritised clean information delivery, precise component alignment, and typographic confidence over visual complexity. Every element earns its presence by communicating something specific about the company's capability or scope.

Depth on Demand

Different visitors require different levels of information depth. The visual hierarchy was engineered so that a scanning visitor could assess the company's scope in seconds, while a serious evaluator could drill into detailed service specifications, project histories, and operational credentials without the platform forcing that depth on a visitor who wasn't ready for it.

UX & Experience Design

Designed for the Decision-Maker Who Has Twelve Tabs Open

Corporate B2B visitors are not leisurely browsers. They are task-oriented, time-pressured, and comparing your platform against competitors with identical intent. The UX architecture for Cemex Project Integrated was built around this reality — making it possible for a procurement evaluator to understand the company's full capability and reach the contact mechanism in under ninety seconds, while simultaneously providing the structural depth for a visitor who wanted to spend fifteen minutes in detailed research.

Homepage: Rapid Credibility Assessment

The homepage was engineered to answer the four questions every corporate B2B visitor arrives with: Who are you? What do you do? How large is your operation? How do I start a conversation? The hero section answered the first two. The service overview answered the third. The CTA infrastructure answered the fourth. All four were resolvable above or near the fold on desktop, without requiring navigation or extended scroll. This efficiency communicates operational confidence before a single service page has been read.

Service Discovery: Clear Pathways, No Dead Ends

Service discovery was designed as a two-layer system. Layer one: service category navigation accessible from the homepage and top-level navigation, providing a high-level map of the company's portfolio. Layer two: individual service pages with their own internal structure — scope, deliverables, relevant project context, and contact entry point — allowing a visitor who landed on a specific service page from search to get everything they needed without returning to the homepage. No dead ends. No navigation required to progress.

Mobile: Corporate Experience Without Compromise

Senior decision-makers in the Nigerian market frequently access corporate platforms on mobile during or between meetings. The responsive system was designed to maintain full information depth on mobile — not a stripped-down version of the desktop experience, but an equivalently capable one restructured for a vertical viewport. Navigation collapsed to a structured mobile menu, service grids refolded to single-column card layouts, and contact forms adapted to native mobile input patterns.

Feature Highlights

The Systems That Positioned Cemex Project Integrated as an Enterprise-Grade Digital Operation

Multi-Service Portfolio Architecture

Purpose: A structured service presentation system — service category overview, individual service pages, and cross-service navigation — designed to make a multi-vertical portfolio comprehensible without overwhelming visitors with information density.

Service clarity is directly linked to inquiry quality. When prospective clients can clearly identify which service category applies to their need, the inquiries that reach the company's team are better-qualified and easier to convert.

Project Portfolio Showcase

Purpose: A filterable project gallery presenting completed works by service category, scale, and geography — giving prospective clients evidence of operational capability rather than claims about it.

In infrastructure services, proof precedes trust. A portfolio section that shows completed projects at relevant scale is more effective than any marketing copy at convincing an institutional client that the company can handle their project scope.

Corporate Inquiry & Lead Management System

Purpose: Structured contact infrastructure with service-specific inquiry routing — matching incoming contact form submissions to the relevant service team and capturing project scope information to facilitate faster, more qualified follow-up.

A generic "contact us" form is a friction point in B2B service sales. Service-specific routing reduces the back-and-forth required before a real project conversation can begin, shortening the sales cycle from initial interest to qualified discussion.

CMS-Managed Service Content

Purpose: WordPress content management architecture with custom post types for services, projects, and team profiles — allowing the company's content team to expand the platform as new service categories, completed projects, and team members are added.

A growing infrastructure company needs a website that grows with it. CMS architecture that allows non-technical content management means the platform reflects the company's current capability rather than its capability at launch date.

Analytics & Performance Monitoring

Purpose: Visitor behaviour tracking with service page engagement metrics, contact form event monitoring, and traffic source attribution — providing the business with data to understand which services are generating the most interest and from what sources.

Corporate websites that don't generate performance data can't be optimised. Analytics visibility transforms the platform from a static asset into an intelligence tool that informs both content strategy and service prioritisation.

SEO-Ready Information Architecture

Purpose: Semantic HTML structure, service-specific page meta, structured data markup, and URL architecture designed for visibility in construction, procurement, and facility management search queries in the Nigerian market.

Corporate platforms that aren't search-visible depend entirely on referral and direct traffic for discovery. SEO architecture extends the company's reach to prospective clients actively searching for the services it provides — creating an acquisition channel that compounds over time.

Technical Execution

An Enterprise Stack Built for Long-Term Scalability and Content Independence

The technology choices for Cemex Project Integrated were governed by two requirements that operate in some tension with each other: the platform needed to be technically sophisticated enough to deliver a genuinely enterprise-grade experience, while simultaneously being operationally simple enough that the company's internal team could manage and expand the content without requiring ongoing developer involvement. Resolving that tension required separating the presentation layer (where sophistication was required) from the content management layer (where simplicity was required).

Frontend Architecture

  • React.js component architecture — reusable UI components for service cards, project tiles, and team profiles, ensuring visual consistency across the platform as content is added
  • HTML5 semantic structure — service pages built with appropriate heading hierarchy and structured content blocks for both accessibility and search engine comprehension
  • Performance-optimised asset pipeline — images compressed and format-converted, CSS and JavaScript bundled and minified, with critical styles inlined to accelerate initial render
  • Responsive layout system — CSS Grid and Flexbox-based layout that adapts cleanly across desktop, tablet, and mobile without media-query fragmentation

Backend & CMS

  • WordPress CMS with custom post types — services, projects, and team profiles structured as distinct content models with their own field sets and editorial workflows
  • ACF (Advanced Custom Fields) integration — flexible content components allowing the editorial team to build varied page layouts without touching code
  • Server-side form processing — contact form submissions handled securely with validation, spam filtering, and email routing to the appropriate service team

Infrastructure & Integrations

  • Cloud hosting with CDN delivery — static assets distributed globally, reducing load times for Nigerian users accessing the platform on variable connectivity
  • Google Analytics 4 — full event tracking with service page engagement metrics, scroll depth, and form submission events configured for ongoing content performance measurement
  • Schema.org structured data — LocalBusiness and Service markup implemented for enhanced search engine understanding of the company's services and geographic scope
  • SEO infrastructure — XML sitemap generation, robots.txt configuration, and canonical URL structure to ensure correct indexation of the multi-page service architecture

Stack: React.js · HTML5 · CSS3 · WordPress CMS · PHP · CDN · Google Analytics 4 · Schema.org Markup · ACF · Contact Routing System · Performance Asset Pipeline
